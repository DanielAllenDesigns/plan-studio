# Chapter 15: Glossary

Residential design and drafting terms as Plan Studio and Chief Architect use them,
in plain language. Terms are alphabetical. "See 4.1" points to a section of this manual.

## A

**Active layer, Active Layer Display Options.** The panel that lists the plan's layers and lets you turn
each on or off (Disp) or lock it (Lock). See 5.5.

**Alt key.** In Plan Studio, holding Alt while drawing a wall turns off the 15 degree angle snap. Option
on a Mac.

**Angle snap.** A drawing aid that makes a line fall on a neat angle, here every 15 degrees from the last
point (so 0, 15, 30 ... 90). See 2.3.

**Area, Interior / Standard / Centerline.** Three ways to measure a room. Interior Area is the floor
between the inside faces of the walls. Standard Area runs to the outside of the exterior walls and the middle of
shared walls. Centerline area runs to the middle of every wall. See 4.1.

**Attic wall.** A short wall in the attic, usually under the roof, that holds up the roof or defines a space. A
floor of kind Attic sits above the top floor.

## B

**Backsplash.** The strip of material behind a countertop, protecting the wall from splashes. Default
height in the cabinet dialog is 0 until you set one.

**Baseline.** In roof work, the line along the top outer edge of a wall where a roof plane begins (the eave
line). In dimensioning, the line all measurements are taken from.

**Base cabinet.** A floor-standing kitchen or bath cabinet with a countertop on top. 24" deep and 36" tall,
including the counter, in Daniel's template.

**Bay window.** A window that projects out from the wall in angled sections. (Planned.)

**Bearing wall.** A wall that carries the weight of the floor or roof above it, not just its own.

**Bumping.** A rule that makes a moved cabinet stop against a wall or slide up against its neighbor instead of
overlapping it.

## C

**CAD.** Computer-aided drafting: plain lines, arcs, circles, text. In Chief and Plan Studio, "CAD objects"
are these 2D drawing items, as opposed to model objects such as walls.

**CAD block.** A group of CAD objects saved or kept together so they move as one thing.

**Callout.** A piece of text with a leader line that points at something and explains it.

**Casing.** The trim around a door or window opening on the wall surface. Defaults here are 3 1/2" interior
and 3 1/4" exterior.

**Ceiling height.** The distance from the finished floor to the finished ceiling. 9'-1 1/8" (109 1/8") in
Daniel's template, which is 9 feet of room plus the thickness of the floor structure above it.

**Centerline.** The middle of a wall, drawn as one line. Plan Studio stores each wall by its centerline.

**Clerestory.** A row of windows high on a wall, above eye level, to bring in light. (No special tool.)

**Conditioned space.** A room that is heated or cooled. Garages, porches and crawl spaces are usually
unconditioned.

**Contour (line).** A line on a site plan joining points at the same elevation. Closer lines mean a steeper slope.

**Crawl space.** A low space under the first floor, too short to stand in, between the ground and the floor.

**Cripple (stud).** A short stud above or below a window or door opening.

**Cross section.** A drawing of the building as if sliced through, showing the inside from the cut. See 10.2.

## D

**Dimension.** A measurement drawn on the plan with extension lines, a dimension line and a value. **Temporary
dimension**: a measurement that appears while you draw or select something and is never saved.

**Doll house view.** A 3D view with the ceilings and roof hidden so you can look into the rooms from above.

**Door types.** Hinged (swings on hinges), doorway (a cased opening with no door), sliding, pocket (slides into the wall),
bifold, barn, garage, fixed and shower. Only hinged is built so far.

**Dormer.** A small structure that sticks out of a sloped roof and holds a vertical window. (Planned.)

## E

**Eave.** The lower edge of a sloped roof, where it hangs past the wall. See **overhang**.

**Egress.** A way out. An egress window in a bedroom must be big enough to climb through in an emergency. The
code limits are in 4.8.

**Elevation.** Two meanings. (1) A drawing of one side of the building, seen straight on, as in "front elevation".
(2) The height of a point above a reference, as in a terrain "elevation point".

**Exterior wall.** A wall between inside and outside. It carries siding or stucco on one face and has a roof directive.

## F

**Face frame.** A frame of narrow boards on the front of a cabinet box that the doors attach to. The other style is
**frameless**. See 6.3.

**Fascia.** The board along the edge of the roof eave where a gutter is fastened. Nominal 6" in `plan-roof`.

**Filler.** A narrow strip that closes a gap between a cabinet and a wall or another cabinet. (Planned.)

**Fill style.** The pattern or color a closed shape is filled with in plan, such as a hatch.

**Floor platform.** The structure of a floor: finish, subfloor and joists. Its thickness (10 1/4" by default) adds
to the ceiling height to give the floor-to-floor rise.

**Foundation.** What the building sits on. Types in Plan Studio: **walls with footings** (a stem wall on a wider
footing), **monolithic slab** (one concrete pour with thickened edges) and **piers** (posts under the building).

**Footing.** The wide concrete base under a foundation wall that spreads the load into the ground.

**Framing.** The wood or steel skeleton of a building: studs, joists, rafters, headers. See 14.2 (`plan-framing`).

**Frieze.** A trim board where the top of the wall meets the soffit under the eave.

**Full camera.** A 3D view from a person's eye height, 66", looking in a chosen direction. See 10.2.

## G

**Gable.** The triangular part of an end wall under a sloping roof. A **gable roof** has two slopes meeting at a ridge,
with a gable at each end.

**Gambrel.** A roof with two slopes on each side, the lower steeper (a barn roof). (Planned.)

**GFCI.** Ground-fault circuit interrupter: an outlet that cuts power fast to prevent shock. Required near water.

**Grid, reference grid and snap grid.** The reference grid is the visible background lines (12" in Daniel's
template). The snap grid is the invisible spacing the cursor jumps to (1").

## H

**Header.** A beam over a door or window opening that carries the load of the wall above it.

**Hip roof.** A roof that slopes on all four sides. A **hip** is the sloping line where two roof planes meet at an outside corner.

**Hinged door.** The ordinary door that swings open on hinges. `D, H`.

## I

**Interior wall.** A wall inside the building, usually a partition that does not carry the roof.

**IRC.** International Residential Code, the model building code Plan Check measures against (2021 edition).

## J

**Jamb.** The vertical side of a door or window opening, or the frame lining the opening.

**Joist.** One of the parallel horizontal beams that hold up a floor or ceiling.

## K

**King stud, trimmer.** Studs beside an opening: the king runs full height; the trimmer (jack) is shorter and holds
the header.

**Knee wall.** A short wall, often under a sloping roof, that supports the roof or edges an attic room.

## L

**Landing.** A flat platform in a stair where it turns or rests.

**Layer.** A named group of objects with shared display settings: on or off, locked, color, line weight. Walls, Doors
and Dimensions each have their own. See 5.5.

**Layer set.** A saved combination of which layers are on, locked and how they look, so a view can show only what it needs. A **saved plan view** (such as "Floor Plan View") pairs a layer set with a floor and a zoom. See 5.5.

**Layout.** In Chief, the part of the program that arranges views, schedules and notes on sheets of paper for printing. See 11.5.

**Lintel.** A beam or trim piece over an opening.

**Lite.** One pane of glass in a window or door. "Lites across and vertical" counts the panes. A **muntin** is the thin bar between lites.

**Living area.** The part of the house counted as livable space. Garages, porches, decks and crawl spaces are normally excluded. See 4.6.

## M

**Main layer.** The structural layer of a wall (the framing). Other layers (siding, sheathing, drywall) are measured from it. Exactly one layer per wall type.

**Mansard.** A roof with two slopes on every side, the lower nearly vertical. (Planned.)

**Marquee.** The rectangle you drag on empty space to select several objects.

**Miter.** A corner joint cut at half the angle between two parts. Wall corners are mitered so the faces meet cleanly.

**Mulled windows.** Two or more windows joined side by side into one unit. (Planned.)

## N

**Niche (wall niche).** A recess in a wall that does not go all the way through. (Planned.)

**Nosing.** The part of a stair tread that sticks out past the riser below it. 1" by default.

**Note.** A numbered annotation from the Text tools, listed in the Note Schedule. (Planned schedule.)

## O

**Object snap.** A drawing aid that pulls the cursor to a useful spot on an existing object: an endpoint, midpoint, intersection, perpendicular foot or point on a wall.

**Opening.** A door or window (or doorway) cut into a wall. Each opening belongs to one wall.

**Option strip.** The row of small buttons a tool draws at the top of the canvas to choose its mode. See 5.1.

**Overhang.** How far a roof or countertop sticks out past what holds it up. Roof: 16" by default. Countertop: 1".

## P

**Partition.** An interior wall that divides space. In the cabinet tools, a vertical panel.

**Pier.** A post, often concrete, that holds up part of the building above the ground.

**Pitch.** The slope of a roof, written as rise over 12 of run. An 8:12 roof rises 8 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal distance.

**Plate.** The horizontal board at the bottom (sole plate) or top (top plate) of a framed wall that the studs fit between.

**Plan view.** A view looking straight down on a floor, with the roof removed: the usual drawing for a floor plan.

**Pony wall.** A short wall, often waist high; or a wall of two types stacked, a lower and an upper. (Planned.)

**Pocket door.** A door that slides into a hollow space inside the wall. (Planned.)

## R

**Rafter.** A sloping beam that carries the roof from the ridge down to the wall.

**Rake.** The sloping edge of a gable roof at the end of the building.

**Reference display.** A view setting that shows the floor below (or above) in gray behind the current one, so you can line walls up. (Toggle exists; drawing is planned.)

**Reveal.** A small step back, such as the 1/4" between a door jamb edge and its casing.

**Ridge.** The top line where two roof slopes meet. A **ridge cap** covers it.

**Riser.** The vertical part of a stair step, or the height of one step.

**Room divider.** An invisible wall that closes a room without showing as a wall.

**Rough opening.** The framed hole in a wall that a door or window sits in, slightly bigger than the unit.

## S

**Schedule.** A table that lists objects of one kind with their sizes: door schedule, window schedule, room schedule. See 11.2.

**Section.** See **cross section**.

**Shed roof.** A roof with a single slope.

**Sill.** The bottom of a window opening (the sill height is measured from the floor), or the board that sits on top of a foundation (sill plate).

**Skylight.** A window set into a sloping roof.

**Slab.** A flat concrete floor poured on the ground.

**Soffit.** The underside of an overhang, or a boxed-in drop above cabinets or in a ceiling.

**Space planning.** Laying out rooms by size and adjacency before drawing walls. See 4.7.

**Specification dialog.** Chief's name for the dialog you open by double-clicking an object, with tabs for every setting.

**Stem wall.** A short foundation wall that rises from the footing to the floor.

**Stringer.** The sloped board under a stair that carries the treads.

**Stud.** One of the vertical boards in a framed wall, usually at 16" on center.

## T

**T-junction.** Where one wall ends against the side of another. The corner join is an L; a crossing is an X.

**Tread.** The flat part of a stair step you stand on. 10" deep or more by the code.

**Toe kick.** The recessed strip at the bottom of a base cabinet so you can stand close. 4" high, 3" deep by default.

**Truss.** A pre-built triangulated frame, usually used for roofs. (Planned.)

## V

**Valley.** The inside corner where two roof slopes meet and water runs together.

**Vaulted ceiling.** A ceiling that follows the slope of the roof, higher in the middle. (Ceiling planes are planned.)

## W

**Wall type.** A named recipe for a wall's layers and thickness, such as Stucco-6: stucco, sheathing, 5 1/2" framing, drywall. See 2.7.

**Winder.** A pie-shaped stair tread that lets a stair turn a corner without a landing.
